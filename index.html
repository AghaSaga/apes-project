MISSION BRIEFING

Meet one of the rarest wild canids on Earth.

The red wolf is listed as Critically Endangered by the IUCN and Endangered under the United States Endangered Species Act.

WILD RANGE TODAY Eastern North Carolina

STATION 1

Know your specimen.

Red wolves have long legs, tall pointed ears, and a lean build. Their coat can mix cinnamon red, tan, gray, brown, and black. Adults are about 4 to 5 feet long from nose to tail and usually weigh about 40 to 80 pounds.

They are generally smaller than gray wolves but larger and heavier built than coyotes.

STATION 2

Enter the habitat.

Historically, red wolves lived across much of the eastern and south central United States. Today, the only wild population lives in eastern North Carolina.

Useful habitat includes forests, wetlands, marshes, and coastal prairie with enough prey, water, cover, and connected space to move.

STATION 4

What is pushing them toward extinction?

1

Human caused mortality

Gunshots and vehicle strikes can have a major effect on such a small wild population.

2

Coyote hybridization

When red wolf numbers are low and breeding partners are lost, pairing with coyotes becomes more likely.

3

Habitat change

Development and habitat alteration reduce the connected space wolves need to hunt, den, and find mates.

FINAL MISSION

Save the pack.

These are real strategies used in red wolf recovery.

1

Managed releases

Carefully selected red wolves from the SAFE population can be released into eastern North Carolina to strengthen the wild population.

2

Pup fostering

Very young captive born pups can be placed into wild dens so they are raised by wild red wolf parents.

3

Tracking and monitoring

Tracking collars help biologists follow survival, movement, breeding pairs, and threats.

4

Protect habitat

Recovery depends on enough connected habitat and fewer preventable human caused deaths.
